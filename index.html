Obituary

Joyce Hung Tow Leung, 82, of Honolulu, Hawaii, passed away on September 29, 2026.

Joyce was born in Guangdong, China, in 1944, a time of war and upheaval. Girls were often kept from school, so she spent her early years playing with other children on school grounds, using seeds and whatever else nature provided. In 1953, she came with her family to Hawaii. Her parents later opened a restaurant—first Kinney Chop Suey in Kapahulu and then King Fong Chop Suey in Kaimuki. From seventh grade through college, Joyce took the bus there to work after school and on weekends. She learned early the value of hard work, a lesson she carried with her throughout her life.

At the University of Hawaii, Joyce studied education before marrying Alan H.W. Leung. Together they raised a beautiful family.

Joyce devoted her career to education in Hawaii, teaching Chinese and U.S. history before retiring from Roosevelt High School. She was beloved by her students and often said that teaching was what she did best. When her school’s Chinese language program was threatened, she rallied parents and the community, took her case to the district and the state Board of Education, and succeeded in saving the program. She never backed down from doing what she believed was right for her students, her family, or her community.

Her commitment to education and public service extended well beyond the classroom. For many years, Joyce served on the board of the Pacific and Asian Affairs Council, supporting its mission of helping Hawaii’s students and community better understand the world and the Asia-Pacific region. She also served on the board of Mun Lun School, reflecting her lifelong commitment to Chinese language, culture, and education in Hawaii.

Joyce is survived by her sons, Wayman (Veda) and Truman (Elisabeth); daughter, Grace (Jud); and grandchildren Malia, Owen, Noah, Coco, Eva, Hosanna, Piper, Stephen, and Matthew. She was preceded in death by her husband, Alan, and granddaughter, Jesse.
